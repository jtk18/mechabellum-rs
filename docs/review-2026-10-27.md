# Review, 2026-10-27: whole-match stats, deployment classes, match cards

J asked for a red team and a reviewer group to tear apart the math, setup, charts and layout,
then for a group comparing the website with the desktop client. Seven
agents ran against commit 5cca635:

| Group | Agent | How |
|---|---|---|
| Red | math | Independent Python oracle over the raw replay XML, compared with a disposable server |
| Red | server/API | Hostile requests and crafted replays against a disposable server; the schema-9 upgrade path |
| Red | UI | The website pages and the desktop frontend (in a browser harness) at every viewport |
| Blue | logic | Read `summary.rs`, `detail.rs`, the result rules; Python checks on the replays |
| Blue | server | Read `store.rs`, `games.rs`, `lib.rs`; SQL on a copy of a schema-9 database |
| Blue | UX | Charts, color, wording and accessibility against the dataviz method and its validator |
| Parity | website vs app | Every panel, label, number and default on both, from code and one server's responses |

Every finding below was checked before fixing. Data: the 13 2.0 replays on this Mac (12 of J's
games and the 2324 fixture) and the 473 1.x replays in `personal-replays`. Other players are
named here only by battle ID.

## Fixed (schema 13)

**Results**
- From version 2340, results come only from the listed `roundFightResult`. An entry the list
  doesn't cover, or a value the game doesn't write ("Banana"), is unknown. Before, a list of
  the wrong length fell back to `preRoundFightResult`, which 2340 writes as "Win" for both
  players, so a match could have two winners. Proven by the math red team with a truncated list
  and by the server red team with crafted ones. (`battle_record.rs::match_results`)
- A concession during the last round's deployment (`PAD_GiveUp` before `FinishDeploy`) ends
  the match a fight early: that round is no longer drawn or counted, the player who conceded
  loses, and the final army is the one that fought the round before. Four of J's 1.x replays
  had a phantom last fight. (`last_round_fought`)
- A pre-2340 replay whose last record is round 1 has no fight to judge by; it was two winners.
- Validation now checks what the 2340 list claims: a lost fight costs reactor HP and a won one
  doesn't (110 of 110 fights in the 9 real 2340 replays), the two players' lists oppose, the list
  covers the fights played, and every value is one the game writes. A loser flipping their
  results to claim the win was accepted before (`docs/upload-trust.md` listed the check; it
  didn't exist). No real replay on hand is flagged.

**Numbers**
- Techs leave out nothing: the last fight's techs add that round's research (`UpgradeTechnology`
  net of undo). They were missing in 20 of 26 player-games; the old `player_techs` table had the
  same gap. Control test: the research added to a round's starting techs equals the next record,
  in every round with a next record.
- Level-ups count upgrades bought (`UpgradeUnit` net of undo), not levels units arrived with:
  443 counted against 328 real upgrades before.
- Units added and sold are `None` unless the fights run from the first without a gap (the 2324
  fixture had added - sold - final = -10).
- AsyncBattle rounds are classed on their own half each fight.
- Flank units are drawn turned on every 2.0 board, not only classified ones.

**Counting and display**
- Deployment tables say "Results" (player results), not "Matches": with both players in the
  filter a mirror match counted twice. The grid's diagonal shows "mirror · n" when that happens,
  since its rate is 50% by construction. `Matchup` gained `matches` (distinct).
- Under 10 results the deployment table shows the record, not a bar, as the grid already did.
- Rows run front to back in a fixed order everywhere.
- Band colors are one ordinal ramp (light to dark, front to back) in a hue no side, win or loss
  color uses. Before, backboard was the same blue as "you", "win" and pick rate.
- Website card: the headline is who won (reactor HP decides a match), with fights won beside it;
  "Fight N of M" above the board; draws marked D on the tape; flank columns from fight 2; the
  2v2/brawl table read a removed field and showed only dashes.
- Desktop: the match view opens on round 1; the round label counts fights like the tape; towers
  are drawn; band guides are readable (13px labels, 1px strokes); unclassified tape bars and the
  smaller tale bars are visible; the tooltip is solid, opens on keyboard focus and hides on
  scroll; dates show in UTC (they read a day early west of UTC); the unit panel says HP "going
  in", and a draw isn't "Lost".
- Wording: sym/asym is "three quarters or more of that band's ground on one side", standard is
  defined as ground and as the fallback class, and "the line" is defined.

**From the UI red team** (each reproduced in a browser first)
- Website: "Show more" ignores a second click while a page loads (a double click duplicated a
  page and skipped the next for good); a response for filters that have since changed is
  dropped (old results showed under new filters on both pages); arrow keys on the fight tape
  move from the focused fight, and the tape is one tab stop; captions wrap and the stats grids
  fit at 320 and 390 wide, the matchup grid's row labels staying put; the units-used win bars
  are centered on the filtered share of winners (47% under all modes, where brawls have three
  losers per winner), not 50%; the length chart shows lengths with no matches; the band stripes
  are visible and edged; a grey "Not classed" is in the legend; the tale of the tape reads once
  per row to a screen reader; "won/lost/drew", "mean per player result".
- Names from uploads are isolated (U+2068/2069 in composed text, `unicode-bidi: isolate` on name
  elements): a name with U+202E turned the sentences around it backwards.
- The lean reads "their left/right", from the player's own view: the site draws north up and
  the app turns the board, so "right side" named opposite sides on the two.
- Desktop: the fight tape sits above the board, so picking a fight changes the board in view;
  one player order throughout (top of the board and tape = left of the tale and units used);
  names in the match list keep at least 80px (they were cut to 3 letters at 1000px); a long name
  wraps instead of pushing the page sideways; band labels draw over the units with a halo;
  "Back to matches" returns to where the list was scrolled.

**Server**
- A rebuild no longer loses or revives matches stored or removed while it runs (the desktop app
  rebuilds in the background while its watcher stores replays), and one record that won't insert
  is logged and skipped instead of stopping every command. A replay whose blob outlived its match
  row can be stored again.
- Ratings outside 0..1,000,000 are refused: i64::MAX and i64::MIN overflowed the MMR difference
  and broke `/api/stats` for everyone.
- A gzipped upload inflates to at most 16 MB and is refused past it (413); a 262 KB gzip had
  forced a 256 MB allocation. Every stored document is capped at 16 MB, and validation flags an
  army over 300 units in a round.
- 500 responses say "internal error"; the details go to the log.
- The match list carries each side as JSON, not separator characters: a name holding `\x1e`
  (reachable through XML 1.1 character references) forged a second winner with any MMR.
- Cards pass the stored JSON through without re-parsing it; at most 50 per page.
- Derived tables are recreated on upgrade, so a schema-10 database sheds its stale `style`
  column.
- A slip of mine: `docs/TODO.md` named an opponent; removed in 44ca8f6. Commit f185f44's
  message still names them; added to the history-scrub list in J's project doc.

## For J to decide

1. **"Right in front of the towers."** Midboard spans the full board width at that depth; 85%
   of round-1 midboard ground is in units not in either tower's column. Should it be only the
   ground in front of the towers?
2. ~~**"The line."**~~ Settled by J the same day: aggressive is a front edge within 5 squares of
   the zone's front edge. Schema 14.
3. **A "Mixed" class.** 46 of 170 fight deployments are standard only because no band reached
   40%; in 13 of them under a quarter of the ground is standard ground. Also, the bands differ
   in depth (4, 5, 7 and 13 legal rows for a 2x2 unit), which pushes an evenly spread army to
   backboard.
4. **Backboard sym/asym**, and whether a cluster in the middle is "sym". (Standard depth was
   settled the same day: symmetric is "Standard", one-sided "Asym mid aggro".)
5. **Flank columns**: count them as aggressive, or as their own tactic? They open in fight 2
   in the replays (9 units in 6 of 13), while `board.rs`'s board-code check says round 3.
6. **Weighting**: units count by footprint area. Every unit also carries `SellSupply`, which
   would weight by value.
7. **Knife edges**: 98 placements sit exactly on the tower-center row and 56 on the
   midboard edge; 5 of 24 openings flip if one unit moves a square. Splitting each unit's
   ground across the bands it covers (instead of testing one point) would steady that.
8. **Account IDs in public responses.** `/api/replays/{id}` returns them (it did before today),
   and `/api/cards?player_id=` turns an ID into that account's match history. The trust doc says
   IDs stay so players are pseudonymous.
9. **Fingerprints** are built from parser output, so a parser change (today's 2340 fix) makes
   an honest copy disagree with one fingerprinted before. Raw-field fingerprints would leave the
   rebuilt last round uncovered. No copies are stored on the live server yet.

  exactly from earlier records (in-sample for the measured list prices).

## Second round (schema 18)

J asked for another red-team round that checks the numbers against the replays, plus a red and
a blue teamer proposing more stats. Five agents ran against 479f10d: an independent oracle over
the raw XML (red-numbers), a recount of every aggregate under 75 filter combinations on three
databases (red-stats), crafted replays and upgrades against a disposable server (red-hostile),
and the two idea lists. Every finding was checked before fixing.

**Fixed**
- The rebuilt last fight is aligned against the round's moves. A unit card, a specialist that
  grants units, or a summon takes unit indices before the round's purchases, and the replay
  doesn't say how many: each card's or summon's count is now chosen to agree with the most
  `MoveUnit` facts, a purchase skips indices already taken, a move places a purchase that has no
  buy position, and skills gained mid-round (skill cards, blueprints named after a skill) add
  skill slots. Control, rebuilding every round that has a next record: 2.0 184 of 184 rounds
  exact (was 173), 1.x 5,944 of 6,156 (was 5,746), wrong units 286 of 93,671 (was 579). Two of
  J's last fights had phantom units: 20261027--67346621 now 26 units and 3,700 supply (was 28,
  3,900), 20261022--201727720 34 and 4,300 (was 35, 4,450).
- 1.x purchases the replay never places are kept in the army, its value and counts; they're
  only left off the board and out of the bands. 20241206--134297685: 36 units, 6,600 (was 34).
- Crafted positions, levels and sell values can't overflow anything: positions are clamped to
  ±100,000, levels to 1,000, and a sell value over 2,000 counts as unknown. A debug build
  panicked at four sites; a release build wrapped silently. One upload with every unit at
  4,294,967,295 had moved the winners' mean army value from 3,650 to 286 million.
- A parser panic costs one replay, never a rebuild or a request: ingest, rebuild and reads
  catch it. One poisoned stored replay had stopped the server starting on every schema bump.
- Matches whose replay doesn't say who won leave every stat but stay in the match list (a new
  `settled` flag: one winner in 1v1 and brawls, two in 2v2). Four 1.x Competition replays that
  keep only round 0 and one late round wrote "Win" for both players; before 2340 a result now
  needs the round before the last. Across the 485 replays on hand, 7 matches are unsettled.
- "My opponents" leaves out a 2v2 teammate (it took every other player: 66 rows, 22 teammates).
- Level-ups and cards picked count only replays recorded from fight 1, like units added and
  sold; partial records had pulled the Competition winners' level-ups from 13.3 to 11.2.
- Winners and losers says how many results each row is over when it's fewer than the header.
- Units used shows a record under 10 games (8 of 19 rows read 0% or 100% from one or two), its
  centre no longer falls back to 50% with only winners, and its level column is "Mean top level".
- Dropdown counts match what the stats count (with players, settled, not offline).
- "Won round 1" under 10 results reads "n of m", so a drawn round 1 isn't a loss.
- A replay with no fights has no units-added count, not zero.

**Checked and correct**: every number for a recorded fight matched the oracle (3,210 of 3,210
board entries; labels, leans, results, HP, meetings 196 of 196), every aggregate matched the
recount under all 75 filters, and the knife edges (front edge exactly -60, centre exactly -170,
x = 0, shares exactly 0.40, lean exactly 0.5) agree with the rules.

**Not changed**
- A missing round-1 record starts the match at fight 2 (the 2324 fixture): the data says no
  fight 1 was fought there.
- The match list's round count includes a conceded, unfought round.
- Before 2340, the match result is the next-to-last fight's (the replay doesn't record the
  last); in 81 of 341 1.x 1v1 matches the recorded loser had more HP going into that last fight.
- Army value leaves out upgrades, and points the other way from levels: going into the last
  round the loser had more value in 7 of 12 matches, the winner more levels in 10 of 12.
  Upgrade prices aren't in the replays.

## Verification round (schema 19)

The same red teamers re-ran their checks against 0fa8249. Every earlier finding held as fixed:
the recount agreed on every field over 98 filter runs on four databases (one of them upgraded
from schema 17, byte-identical to a fresh import over 141 responses), and every crafted upload
that had panicked stored cleanly. A schema-13 database with a poisoned replay upgraded, skipped
the poison and rebuilt the rest. Their new findings, fixed:
- Unsettled matches were hidden from the website's Matches page and listed as losses on the
  desktop. Both lists show them now, marked "result unknown"; they still count toward no stat.
- A match with no fight recorded (an offline game ended at once) counted as settled; it isn't.
- The website offered patches and modes with nothing to show (it has no offline toggle).
- A crafted unit index near i64's limit overflowed the rebuild: indices beyond ±1,000,000 are
  ignored now.
- A replay whose mode claims more players than it holds (a 1v1 labelled 2v2) indexed past its
  players in `GameMode::from`, a panic in release builds too.
- The card-and-summon search multiplied with a round's moves: 16,000 crafted moves took 6.7 s.
  Real rounds have at most 44 actions and 90 moves (8,199 rounds); past 1,000 the search is
  skipped. The same file takes 1.0 s now, against 0.5 s with no cards at all.
- Two armies on the same half (only a crafted replay) got a made-up headbutt.

The oracle's re-run (170 of 170 cut 2.0 rounds exact, every field of all 13 2.0 matches agreeing,
both phantom-unit fights now right) found breaks in the new rebuild, fixed in schema 20:
- A crafted round with five cards and 12,000 moved purchases took 85 s to import and 44 s per
  match-page read; the search ceiling above brings both to 2.3 s, the cost of parsing 13 MB.
- A unit card could be counted as giving no units, so a later sell of its unit removed a
  purchase. Every unit card in the 2.0 replays took at least one index; the minimum is one.
- Any skill released on no unit counted as a possible summon, and past five they were silently
  cut from the search, misplacing what followed. Only summoning skills (or ones the replay
  hides) count now, and past five the search is skipped instead of cut short.
- Cards the names table doesn't know (1,888 picks across the 1.x replays) may give units too:
  1.x rebuilds 5,960 of 6,156 rounds exactly (was 5,944).
- Per-unit added and sold were 0 when the match's own counts were unknown; they're null now.

## Draws, byes and watched matches

- A fight where every unit dies is a draw that both players win (J). Upload validation had
  wanted the two players' listed results to oppose, so it would have refused a real replay with
  a draw; a fight both players win is accepted now. A forged win still fails the HP check (a
  winner loses no reactor HP). No 2.0 replay on hand has one; 1.x has 10 fights both players
  won and 15 both drew. The fight tape says so on hover.
- A tournament with an odd number of players gives someone a bye (J). No replay on hand is one,
  so nothing detects it yet; a bye that reads as two winners is unsettled and counts toward no
  stat.
- The four 1.x Competition replays with two "winners" are matches J watched: the game saves
  those too, from the moment watching began. In the desktop app the logged-in account is known
  (from the game's log), and a match it didn't play now leaves every stat, both match lists and
  the dropdown counts; the replay stays stored. Account IDs survive a renamed Steam account:
  kaltorak18 and jtk18 are one ID. With no logged-in account, or one with no stored match, all
  matches count; on the public server shared uploads already need the uploader in the match.

## Stats proposed (for J to pick)

From the red and blue idea agents, merged and ranked by value to a player, confidence in the data
and cost. Sample figures are from J's 12 2.0 matches and the 473 1.x replays.

| Stat | What it shows | Sample | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Comebacks and momentum | Next-fight win after a win; wins from behind on HP | Same player wins the next fight 76% (2.0), 63% (1.x); from 1,000+ HP behind, 29% (2.0) and 42% (1.x) still won | S |
| Which fights decide matches | Match win rate of each fight's winner | Fight 1 winner took the match 49% (1.x) and 7 of 12 (2.0); fight 8, 88% | S |
| Fight margin | HP lost per fight; mechs left standing (`AliveMechCount`) | A lost fight costs 837 HP on average, 526 at fight 1 rising to about 1,250 by fight 7; winners keep a median 27 mechs | S (HP) / M (mechs) |
| Unspent supply | Supply carried over each round | J leaves 7.8 a round, opponents 26.4; in 1.x fight win rate falls from 51% at 0 to 32% at 200+ | M |
| Per-fight deployment win rates | Each fight's class, not only round 1's (7.5x the sample) | Asym mid aggro won 34 of 56 fights; 18 of 24 player-matches changed class | S |
| Card pick rates when offered | 2.0 offers both players the same 4 cards | Additional Deployment Slot picked 7 of 8 times offered; Arclight 0 of 12 | M |
| Upgrades left waiting | Units with a full XP bar not upgraded | Winners left a median 18% waiting, losers 58% | M |
| Starter pack win rates | Packs are recorded per player | 1.x: Fang+Stormcaller 20 of 29, Marksman+Tarantula 19 of 63 | S |
| Picked vs granted specialists | Already in `/api/stats`, never drawn | 1.x: Efficient Giant Manufacturing 167 picks, 44% | S |
| MMR and rank points over time | Desktop "Me" view, from the game log | MMR 1,578 to 1,559 over 10-27's nine games | S |
| Buildings and devices on the board | In the data, drawn nowhere | Defensive Wall in 23 of 24 player-matches | S |
| XP per 100 supply, by unit | The only per-unit combat figure replays hold | Marksman about 330-480, Crawler about 55-80 | M |

Hold for a bigger corpus: unit-against-unit counters (effects of 10-14 points are within noise
at today's sample) and deploy time (not predictive: rounds of 80 s or more won 671 of 1,354).

What misleads now, for J to weigh:
- Army value leaves out upgrades: the loser had more value going into the last round in 7 of
  12 matches, the winner more levels in 10 of 12. Upgrade prices would need the game's tables.
- Level-ups partly follow from winning: upgrades wait on a full XP bar (93%), and fight winners
  earn about twice the XP.
- Final-army unit win rates count mirror matches: Crawler shows 51%, 59% without mirrors.
- "Kept" in units used is about 95% for everything; "Cards picked" is mostly match length.
- Before 2340 the match result is the next-to-last fight's.

## Deferred

- A rebuild holds every parsed replay in memory at once (about 1 MB each), too much for the
  512 MB host once there are a few hundred; parse and insert in chunks.
- A rebuild wipes names a second uploader filled in.
- Card paging is by day then battle ID (stored times are date-only); keyset paging needs a real
  timestamp.
- The rebuilt last fight puts every new unit at level 1 (mean level low by 0.04 on average).
- `player_unit_use` is keyed by unit name; two IDs with one name would lose a row.
- Desktop match list: no filters, so bot games show; same-day order differs from the website.
- The website's heatmap and the app's differ (striping threshold, color steps); "4-player
  Brawl" vs "4-player FFA"; "Firebadger" vs "Fire Badger".
- Real tables (row and cell roles) for the new grids; HP figures visible without a tooltip.
- Desktop tooltips: the tale rows and fight buttons open theirs on focus, but most of the Stats
  view's 367 `data-tip` elements can't take focus.
- Desktop: a failed match-list load leaves "Loading matches…" until the next refresh.
- The reviewers' notes on the starting HP: both players start at 4,500 and differ by -900 to
  +1,200 going into fight 1, from something chosen in rounds 0-1 that no one identified. The
  cards show it faithfully.
